About

I’m a Ph.D. student in Education at the University of California, Irvine, specializing in Teaching, Learning, and Educational Improvement. I work with Nia Nixon in the Language and Learning Analytics Lab.

My research brings together learning analytics, language analysis, and computer-supported collaborative learning. I study what happens to the people in a group when an AI joins the conversation. I’m also one of two principal developers of TRAIL, a platform for studying human–AI teams.

Research & featured work

What changes when an AI becomes part of a team? My work examines the conversation, the experience of belonging, and the tools we use to study both.

01HICSS-60 · Accepted · 2027

The Social Cost of an AI Teammate: How an Artificial Teammate Reshapes Human-Human Communication in Small-Team Decision-Making

Nixon, N., Choi, J., Martins de Bastos, P., Samadi, M. A., Park, S., & JaQuay, S.

An AI can take part in a conversation while the people around it feel less connected. In a text-chat rescue task, students on AI-inclusive teams participated less and reported lower belonging, status, and connection than students on all-human teams.

02Preprint (arXiv)

TRAIL: A Platform for Configurable Human–AI Teaming Experiments

Samadi, M. A., Martins de Bastos, P., Choi, J., JaQuay, S., Park, S., & Nixon, N.

TRAIL lets researchers configure an AI teammate, run group conversations, and connect the discussion with survey responses. Its classroom demonstration showed different patterns for a persona that challenged ideas and one that encouraged inclusion.

Read the paper on arXiv ↗

03Preprint (arXiv)

Out of sync: Accommodation, evaluation, and emergence in five weeks with an agentic AI teammate

Nixon, N., Choi, J., Martins de Bastos, P., & Samadi, M. A.

A teammate can be rated less helpful while the team’s view of its collaboration improves. Across repeated sessions, language, helpfulness ratings, and perceptions of collaboration followed different patterns.
